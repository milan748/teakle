# TEAKLE HANDBOOK
## Brand, Design, Content, Commerce & Reference Authority

**Version:** 1.0

## 00 — Authority

This handbook is the persistent source of truth for Teakle website design and autonomous development.

Authority hierarchy:
1. Teakle brand identity and verified business/product facts
2. Established Teakle website requirements
3. Teakle visual/design language
4. Reference-site principles
5. Agent/developer preference

Reference websites are principles, not templates. They never override Teakle.

## 01 — Brand Foundation

**Brand:** Teakle Atelier  
**Tagline:** Every grain, considered.

Core character:
- quiet luxury
- grounded elegance
- warm tones
- human artistry
- sophistication
- human presence
- refined taste
- unapologetic Indian essence
- teak as the central material
- considered objects rather than generic décor

Brand language should communicate quality through objects, photography and the world around them rather than repeatedly announcing “luxury”, “art” or “craft”.

Core brand line:
> Not furniture. Heirlooms. Made with care and love.

## 02 — Mission & Vision

Mission: bring artistry back into daily living through teakwood pieces that are useful and beautiful, communicating sophistication, good taste and timelessness.

Vision: become India's first teakwood-exclusive luxury home décor brand encompassing statement teakwood décor, sculptures and art pieces.

Treat the vision as a brand ambition, not as independently verified market fact.

## 03 — Product Architecture

### Teakle Essentials
Entry-level / premium handmade utility products such as coasters, bowls and boards.

Characteristics:
- useful
- approachable
- premium
- handmade
- everyday
- lighter and more colourful visual energy

Products are marketed under Teakle rather than flashy sub-brands.

### The Atelier Collection
Premium-to-luxury collectible pieces.

Characteristics:
- singular
- sculptural
- refined
- collectible
- statement-making
- serious
- material-led

Do not present Essentials and Atelier as identical product worlds.

## 04 — Audience

The brand guide identifies the core audience as approximately Indian professionals aged 35–55, senior leadership/founder level, high income, collectors or considered-home buyers, gender-neutral.

The intended mindset is someone who has moved from furnishing a home toward curating it.

Write with confidence and restraint rather than defensive sales persuasion.

## 05 — Voice

Use:
- confident
- direct
- considered
- tasteful
- curated
- grounded
- quietly luxurious
- intimate craft storytelling

Rules:
- Say it once.
- Leave one thing unsaid.
- Vary sentence rhythm.
- Begin with a detail where appropriate.
- State the position; do not defend it.
- Avoid repetitive luxury/premium/artisanal/craftsmanship modifiers.

## 06 — Logo

Use approved Teakle logo assets.

Never:
- redraw/recreate the logo
- fabricate a replacement
- stretch, distort, rotate or skew it
- add strokes, shadows, gradients or bevels
- hand-assemble logo components at arbitrary spacing
- place it on a busy/tight photographic background without separation

The brand guide specifies clear space around the complete lockup using a 100-unit symbol height and 20-unit minimum clear space.

Olive/moss is an accent, not the primary logo colour. Do not make green dominant in Atelier content.

## 07 — Typography

The original brand guide specifies Wasted Vinley, Anonymous Pro and Source Serif Pro.

**Website decision:** the website is standardized to **Instrument Sans only**.

Allowed:
- Regular
- Medium
- SemiBold
- Bold
- Italic
- Bold Italic

Do not introduce serif, script, calligraphic, decorative, novelty or unrelated fonts.

## 08 — Colour

- Espresso: #1C130D
- Ivory: #DDD8D0
- Sandstone: #D8BF9F
- Taupe: #7E6E5B
- Moss: #585A3F

Approximate brand-guide balance:
- Espresso 35%
- Ivory 30%
- Sandstone 18%
- Taupe 12%
- Moss 5%

Espresso and Ivory should operate as two sides of the same system, not as default light background plus occasional brown accent.

Atelier should maintain serious, refined energy and avoid green-heavy treatment.

## 09 — Photography

Preferred:
- soft directional light
- golden-hour/window light
- approximately 30–40° light direction
- organic shadows
- off-centre subjects
- generous negative space
- natural geometry
- macro wood-grain detail
- simple backgrounds
- low-contrast backgrounds
- minimal props
- warm espresso/ivory colour treatment

Avoid:
- catalogue-like symmetry
- prop-heavy styling
- cool casts
- inconsistent grading
- generic imagery where suitable Teakle imagery exists

Atelier imagery: serious, dark, luxurious, often more monochrome.  
Essentials imagery: lighter, more approachable and potentially more colourful.

## 10 — Videography

Prefer:
- slow deliberate camera movement
- static locked frames
- slow push-ins
- documentary/handheld details
- real workshop sounds

Useful process details:
- hands sanding
- shavings falling
- tools catching light

Avoid:
- fast cuts
- whip pans
- speed ramps
- trend-driven audio/effects

Principle: LESS IS MORE.

## 11 — Website Design Language

Teakle should feel:
- architectural
- editorial
- material-led
- restrained
- contemporary
- Indian
- tactile
- premium
- functional

Luxury should come from photography, materiality, scale, proportion, contrast, typography, composition and controlled whitespace.

Do not manufacture luxury through gold effects, decorative fonts, giant buttons, heavy shadows, excessive gradients or empty space for its own sake.

## 12 — Image Composition System

Major informational sections should primarily use:

1. Extreme-left image + text to the right
2. Text to the left + extreme-right image
3. Wide cinematic landscape image

Avoid small centred image + small centred text inside a huge empty canvas.

Major informational imagery should generally be landscape/cinematic rather than tall portrait imagery.

Alternate compositions to create architectural rhythm.

## 13 — Text Width & Whitespace

Philosophy, about and craft text should use a sufficiently wide horizontal field where appropriate.

Whitespace must create hierarchy, pacing, image scale, reading or transition. Reduce dead space that merely slows page momentum.

## 14 — Homepage Structure

Preserve the established structure unless there is concrete evidence for change:

1. Main navigation/header
2. Cinematic hero
3. Announcement strip
4. Philosophy/brand statement
5. Atelier Stories / hero product
6. Craft/making
7. Horizontal collection/category carousel
8. Product collection grid
9. Workshop/making
10. Watch-the-process
11. Footer
12. Mobile navigation

## 15 — Navigation

Desktop should retain:
- correct Teakle logo
- primary navigation
- account
- wishlist
- cart
- existing utility controls

Mobile should retain:
- Home
- Collection
- Wishlist
- Cart
- Account

Use existing routes. Never create fake non-functional controls.

## 16 — Atelier Stories / Hero Product

Treat Atelier products as collectible design objects.

Prioritize:
- large imagery
- material detail
- sculptural presence
- restrained metadata
- product name
- verified price
- functional purchase controls
- wishlist where supported

Luxury presentation does not mean hiding commerce.

## 17 — Carousels

Horizontal collection/category carousels are intentional and must remain.

They should feel:
- editorial
- controlled
- tactile
- horizontally directional
- easy to use

Do not turn them into generic SaaS sliders.

## 18 — Product Grid

The product grid is required.

Preferred desktop:
- 3 columns where appropriate
- consistent image proportions
- clean architectural spacing
- image
- name
- minimal category
- price where appropriate
- wishlist control where applicable

Avoid heavy cards, excessive rounded corners, heavy shadows and badge-heavy UI.

## 19 — Craft / Workshop / Process

Prioritize authentic Teakle material and process:
- teak
- wood grain
- hands
- tools
- workshop
- natural light
- process
- documentary details

Do not invent manufacturing processes or replace authentic imagery with generic workshop stock when suitable Teakle imagery exists.

## 20 — Commerce

Never remove functioning:
- products
- prices
- product pages
- add-to-cart
- cart
- wishlist
- account
- product navigation
- ecommerce routes

Do not change backend/database/authentication/CMS/Shopify architecture during visual refinement unless explicitly instructed.

### Wriver

- contemporary Indian sensibility

### Carl Hansen & Søn

## 22 — Image Selection

Before introducing imagery:
1. Search existing Teakle assets.
2. Prefer authentic Teakle imagery.
3. Prefer fewer strong images over many weak ones.
4. Do not use unrelated stock imagery to fill space.
5. Do not repeat images unnecessarily.
6. Do not alter a product's actual form, colour, material or proportions without explicit instruction.

## 23 — Motion

Prefer:
- subtle entrance transitions
- image reveals
- controlled horizontal movement
- restrained hover states
- smooth carousel interaction

Avoid excessive parallax, bouncing, constant animation and trend-driven effects.

## 24 — Responsive Design

Check desktop, tablet where relevant and mobile.

Pay particular attention to:
- header
- bottom navigation
- image cropping
- typography
- spacing
- carousel usability
- product grid
- CTAs
- overflow
- horizontal scrolling
- touch targets

Mobile is not merely a collapsed desktop.

## 25 — Content Integrity

Never invent:
- product specifications
- dimensions
- prices
- inventory
- materials
- production claims
- testimonials
- awards
- certifications
- statistics
- customer claims
- company history

Use only verified existing information.

## 26 — Autonomous Decision Matrix

| Problem | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Generic result | Reassert Teakle identity |
| Too decorative | Use architectural restraint |
| Product feels commoditized | Use material-first/product-as-object presentation |
| Excessive vertical repetition | Use stronger editorial rhythm |
| Wood feels incidental | Increase material/texture emphasis |
| Excessive dead space | Rebalance proportion and pacing |
| Catalogue-like image | Use off-centre subject, negative space and natural geometry |
| Atelier feels casual | Restore serious/refined/darker energy |
| Essentials feels too severe | Restore approachable/lighter energy |
| Result resembles another brand | Reassert Teakle identity |

## 27 — Non-Negotiable Agent Rules

Never:
- redesign the entire site without evidence
- remove ecommerce functionality
- remove product grids
- remove horizontal carousels
- remove account/wishlist/cart
- replace the logo
- introduce another typeface
- invent content/product data
- introduce arbitrary colours
- introduce unrelated stock imagery
- change backend/CMS/Shopify architecture for visual reasons
- add dependencies for trivial effects
- rewrite working components without need
- optimize for iteration count instead of quality
- push to remote automatically

## 28 — Quality Test

Before accepting a material change:
1. Does it still feel like Teakle?
2. Does it respect the brand identity?
3. Does it improve hierarchy?
4. Does it improve material/product presentation?
5. Is image treatment appropriate?
6. Is typography coherent?
7. Is spacing purposeful?
8. Is functionality preserved?
9. Does it work on mobile?
10. Is it demonstrably better than the previous state?

If the final answer is uncertain, do not make the change.

## 29 — Final Principle

Teakle should feel:

**quiet, material, architectural, contemporary, Indian, tactile, deliberate and collectible.**

It should not feel:

**generic, template-driven, SaaS-like, overly decorative, artificially luxurious, cluttered or empty.**

The objective is not to make Teakle resemble another luxury brand.

The objective is to make Teakle increasingly precise in expressing its own identity.

> **Teakle Atelier. Every Grain, Considered.**

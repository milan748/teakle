Studio

Why we work in solid wood, and why it takes as long as it does.

The materials, the process, and the workshop behind every Teakle piece.

Where We Started

A carpentry practice that became a workshop, over three generations.

Teakle began as a small carpentry practice in India, taking on furniture repair and custom joinery for houses in the area. Over three generations, the same practice narrowed into something more deliberate — fewer commissions, more time per piece, and a refusal to use materials that would not hold up over decades.

We still work the way the workshop always has. A piece is planned by hand, built by hand, and finished by hand. Nothing here is automated because nothing here needed to be.

Materials

Solid wood, and why we don't use anything else.

Why Teak

Teak carries its own natural oils, which is why it has been used in shipbuilding for centuries. It resists moisture and doesn't need synthetic sealants to survive daily use.

Why Solid, Not Veneer

Veneer looks identical on day one and fails first. A solid block can be sanded, repaired, and refinished for generations. A veneer sheet cannot.

Why Grain Matters

Every board is chosen and oriented by hand so the grain runs with the piece's structure, not against it. This is slower, and it's why the piece doesn't crack at the joints.

Why We Let Wood Age

A finished piece will darken and change texture slightly over its first few years. This isn't wear — it's the wood settling into its final state.

Why We Keep Imperfections

A knot or a faint colour shift in the grain isn't sanded away. It's the record of where the tree grew, and it's part of what makes the piece singular.

Why Food-Safe Oil

Lacquer seals moisture in and cracks over time. An oil finish can be reapplied by hand for as long as the piece is in use.

How a Piece Is Made

Selection

Choosing the timber

A single block is chosen for grain and density, then left to dry and settle before any cutting begins.

2–3 Weeks
Joinery

Cutting and fitting

Joints are cut and dry-fitted by hand, checked, and adjusted before any glue or fastener is used.

6–8 Hours
Shaping

Refining the form

Edges and surfaces are shaped and smoothed in stages, by hand, until the proportions feel right in person, not just on paper.

5–6 Hours
Finishing

Oil, not lacquer

Several thin coats of food-safe oil are worked into the grain by hand and left to cure between applications.

4–5 Hours
Inspection

A final pass

The piece is checked by hand for balance, joint tension, and surface consistency before it leaves the workshop.

1 Hour
